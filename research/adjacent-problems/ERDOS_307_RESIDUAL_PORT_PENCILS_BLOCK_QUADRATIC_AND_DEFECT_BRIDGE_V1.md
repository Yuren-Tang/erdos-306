## 1. Purpose

The seeded scalar reduction writes E307 as a residual-`C` port equation

```text
cB-RD(B)=C,
C=x^2p^2,
```

followed by one prime-output test.  This note develops the dual geometry:

1. for fixed `B`, all residual-`C` ports form an affine integer pencil;
2. a residual-one/E313 filling generates the whole residual-`C` pencil;
3. primitive coprimality on the pencil is exactly controlled by one scalar parameter;
4. for fixed E307 prefixes and right block `B`, the seed prime `p` satisfies one explicit
   quadratic equation;
5. E307 derivative cycles are paired equal-and-opposite derivative defects, with the
   consecutive case sitting at the Giuga/primary-pseudoperfect intersection.

## 2. The full port pencil of one block

Let `B>1` be squarefree and put

```text
d=D(B).
```

Then

```text
gcd(B,d)=1.                                    (2.1)
```

Fix an integer residual `C`.  Consider

```text
cB-Rd=C.                                       (2.2)
```

### Theorem 2.1 — affine pencil of all ports

If `(c_*,R_*)` is one integer solution of `(2.2)`, then every integer solution is uniquely

```text
c=c_*+t d,
R=R_*+t B,
t in Z.                                        (2.3)
```

#### Proof

The displayed family preserves `(2.2)`.  Conversely, the difference of two solutions satisfies

```text
(c-c_*)B=(R-R_*)d.
```

Since `gcd(B,d)=1`, one has `d|(c-c_*)` and `B|(R-R_*)`, with the same quotient `t`. `square`

The normalized slope is

```text
c/R=d/B+C/(RB).                                (2.4)
```

Thus every positive-residual pencil approaches the arithmetic logarithmic derivative `d/B`
from above as `R` grows.

## 3. Scaling a residual-one filling

Suppose `B` fills a residual-one port `(R_0,c_0)`:

```text
c_0B-R_0d=1.                                   (3.1)
```

### Corollary 3.1 — residual-`C` pencil generated by one E313 block

All integer residual-`C` ports filled by the same block `B` are

```text
c=Cc_0+t d,
R=CR_0+t B,
t in Z.                                        (3.2)
```

Hence every known residual-one filling supplies an explicit family for every residual `C`, not
only for `C=1`.

### Theorem 3.2 — exact primitive gcd formula

For the pencil `(3.2)`,

```text
gcd(c,R)=gcd(C,t).                              (3.3)
```

#### Proof

Any common divisor of `C` and `t` divides both entries in `(3.2)`.  Conversely, let `g` divide
`c` and `R`.  Then `g|cB-Rd=C`.  Also

```text
cR_0-Rc_0
 =t(dR_0-Bc_0)
 =-t,
```

using `(3.1)`.  Thus `g|t`, so `g|gcd(C,t)`. `square`

Therefore

```text
(c,R) is primitive iff gcd(C,t)=1.              (3.4)
```

The residual-one block creates primitive residual-`C` ports for every parameter avoiding the
finitely many prime divisors of `C`.

## 4. Matching an E313 block to an E307 seed

Use coprime squarefree E307 prefixes `x,y`, with

```text
a=D(x),
b=D(y),
Delta=xy-ab>0.                                  (4.1)
```

For a new left seed prime `p`, the seeded port is

```text
c_p=Delta p-xb,
R_p=y(ap+x),
C_p=x^2p^2.                                     (4.2)
```

If a fixed residual-one block `B` fills `(R_0,c_0)`, then it can serve as the right E307 block
at seed `p` precisely when some integer `t` satisfies

```text
Delta p-xb=x^2p^2c_0+tD(B),
y(ap+x)=x^2p^2R_0+tB.                           (4.3)
```

The two equations are compatible exactly when the seeded port equation holds.  The pencil
viewpoint is useful because it converts the matching problem into intersection of:

```text
one explicit E307 seed curve in (c,R,C),
and one residual-C port pencil generated by B.
```

No claim is made that an arbitrary known E313 block has such an intersection.

## 5. Block-first quadratic for the seed prime

A more direct dualization fixes a squarefree right block `B`, disjoint from `xy`, and puts

```text
d=D(B).
```

The seeded port equation is

```text
(Delta p-xb)B-y(ap+x)d=x^2p^2.                 (5.1)
```

### Theorem 5.1 — seed quadratic

The block `B` can be the right filler of a seeded E307 completion only if the seed `p` is a root
of

```text
x^2p^2
 -(Delta B-ay d)p
 +x(bB+yd)=0.                                  (5.2)
```

Conversely, if a new prime `p` satisfies `(5.2)` and

```text
Delta p-xb>0,                                  (5.3)
```

then the scalar port equation holds.  The associated positive integer

```text
r=(yB-xp)/D(xp)                                (5.4)
```

is automatic; an E307 cycle results exactly when `r` is a new prime and all factors are
disjoint.

#### Proof

Equation `(5.2)` is a rearrangement of `(5.1)`.  The automatic integrality and positivity of
`r` follow from the general seeded residual-port theorem. `square`

### Corollary 5.2 — block discriminant

Define

```text
mathfrakD_B
 =(Delta B-ay d)^2-4x^3(bB+yd).                (5.5)
```

There are at most two rational seed candidates:

```text
p={Delta B-ay d +- sqrt(mathfrakD_B)}/(2x^2).  (5.6)
```

An integer seed requires:

1. `mathfrakD_B` to be a nonnegative square;
2. the numerator in `(5.6)` to be divisible by `2x^2`;
3. the resulting value to be a new prime satisfying `(5.3)`.

If both roots are integers, Vieta gives the additional filters

```text
p_1+p_2=(Delta B-ay d)/x^2,
p_1p_2=(bB+yd)/x.                               (5.7)
```

In particular,

```text
x^2 | Delta B-ayD(B),
x   | bB+yD(B).                                 (5.8)
```

Thus fixed-block construction is finite before primality: every block gives at most two seeds.

## 6. Seed-first and block-first duality

The two established reductions are dual:

```text
SEED-FIRST:
  fix p;
  factor the cubic norm K_p;
  enumerate right two-prime blocks or grow a scalar port tree.

BLOCK-FIRST:
  fix B;
  test one quadratic discriminant mathfrakD_B;
  obtain at most two seed primes p.
```

This suggests meet-in-the-middle searches in which blocks are generated by residual-port
composition while prefix/seed states are indexed by their quadratic coefficients.

## 7. Arithmetic-derivative defect coordinates

Define the derivative defect

```text
epsilon(n)=D(n)-n.                              (7.1)
```

### Theorem 7.1 — equal-and-opposite defect characterization

Let `x<y` be coprime squarefree integers and put

```text
h=y-x>0.
```

Then

```text
D(x)=y,
D(y)=x                                         (7.2)
```

if and only if

```text
epsilon(x)=h,
epsilon(y)=-h.                                 (7.3)
```

Thus every E307 cycle is a pair of squarefree integers whose arithmetic-derivative defects are
equal and opposite, with magnitude exactly their additive gap.

This is a second global coordinate system for E307, complementary to reciprocal products and
bilateral ports.

## 8. Consecutive cycles: Giuga meets primary pseudoperfect

Take `h=1`, so `y=x+1`.  Then `(7.3)` becomes

```text
D(x)=x+1,
D(x+1)=x=(x+1)-1.                              (8.1)
```

For squarefree composite integers:

- `D(x)=x+1` is the arithmetic-derivative form of the Giuga condition;
- `D(y)=y-1` is exactly the primary-pseudoperfect condition.

### Corollary 8.1 — consecutive E307 bridge

A consecutive pair `x,x+1` forms an E307 arithmetic-derivative two-cycle if and only if:

```text
x is squarefree and satisfies D(x)=x+1,
x+1 is squarefree and primary pseudoperfect.    (8.2)
```

Equivalently, a consecutive E307 cycle is a consecutive Giuga/primary-pseudoperfect pair.

This does not assert that such a pair exists.  It identifies an exact intersection of the two
adjacent arithmetic-derivative theories.

## 9. Computational sanity check

A Wolfram-language enumeration up to `200000` found:

```text
D(n)=n+1, squarefree:
  30, 858, 1722, 66198;

D(n)=n-1, squarefree:
  2, 6, 42, 1806, 47058.
```

No first-list element had its successor in the second list.  This is only a small sanity check
and has no exclusion force for consecutive or general E307 cycles.

## 10. Research consequences

The current construction programme has three compatible engines:

1. **residual-one block reuse:** generate residual-`C` port pencils from known or newly
   constructed E313 fillings;
2. **block-first quadratic:** rank blocks by square or near-square `mathfrakD_B`;
3. **seed-first port tree:** grow right blocks while monitoring the final prime output.

The pencil gcd theorem removes one potential technical obstacle: primitive ports are abundant
along every pencil whenever the parameter is chosen coprime to `C`.

The true remaining coupled difficulty is simultaneous occurrence of:

```text
one residual-C block filling,
one admissible seed prime,
one final affine prime output.
```
