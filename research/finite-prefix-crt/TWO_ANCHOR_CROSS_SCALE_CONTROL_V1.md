This note attacks the remaining `O(1/log Z)` reciprocal-load bottleneck in the
finite-prefix candidate.

The full global chain of high dyadic control blocks is replaced by only two
anchor blocks:

1. the lowest high block at scale `X=sqrt(Z/2)`;
2. the top prime block at scale `Z`.

All other prefix coordinates are eliminated by one sparse actual-prime sensor
sample from the top block.  The two anchors retain internal complete graphs and
one cross-scale complete bipartite graph.  Their total reciprocal load is
`O(1/(log Z)^2)`, while the lowest anchor still supplies variance
`asymp 1/(Z(log Z)^2)`.

The new mathematical ingredient is a cross-scale label-synchronization theorem:
if the two anchor blocks carry different coherent labels, the top block acts as
a high-energy inverse sensor for at least one lowest-block prime and forces a
penalty of order `Z/log Z`.

The route gives a candidate sharpening

```text
gamma_N = O(1/(log p_N)^2),
```

conditional only on packaging the already verified single-block SBEE mechanism
in the exact local coherent/floor interface stated in Section 6.

## 1. Two anchor blocks

Put

\[
 Z=2^{2k+1},\qquad X=2^k=\sqrt{Z/2}.
\]

Let

\[
 A=\{p:X\le p<2X,\ p\text{ prime}\},
\]

and

\[
 B=\{q:Z/2\le q<Z,\ q\text{ prime}\}.
\]

Use the control edge family

\[
 E_{\rm anch}
 =\binom A2\ \cup\ (A\times B)\ \cup\ \binom B2.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Its reciprocal load is

\[
 \Lambda_{\rm anch}
 =\frac12\left(\sum_{p\in A}\frac1p\right)^2
  +\left(\sum_{p\in A}\frac1p\right)
   \left(\sum_{q\in B}\frac1q\right)
  +\frac12\left(\sum_{q\in B}\frac1q\right)^2
  +o(k^{-2}).
\]

By reciprocal-prime Mertens on fixed-ratio intervals,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda_{\rm anch}=O(k^{-2}).
 }
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The inverse-square load is dominated by the internal lowest-anchor graph:

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_{\rm anch}^2
 :=\sum_{e\in E_{\rm anch}}\frac1{e^2}
 \asymp\frac1{Zk^2}.
 }
 \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus

\[
 \sigma_{\rm anch}^{-1}\asymp\sqrt Z\,k.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

This is the same coherent-label scale needed in the original full global-control
construction, but at reciprocal cost `O(k^-2)` rather than `O(k^-1)`.

## 2. Cross-scale CRT phase identity

For primes `p in A`, `q in B` and integers `u,v`, let `n_{p,q}(u,v)` be the
centered CRT representative satisfying

\[
 n_{p,q}(u,v)\equiv u\pmod p,
 \qquad
 n_{p,q}(u,v)\equiv v\pmod q.
\]

Put `Delta=u-v`.  Modulo one,

\[
 \frac{n_{p,q}(u,v)}{pq}
 \equiv
 \frac{v}{pq}
 +\frac{\Delta q^{-1}}p.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed, write `n=v+qt`; then

\[
 t\equiv(u-v)q^{-1}\pmod p.
\]

This identity turns the entire top block into an inverse sensor for the label
difference `Delta` at each lowest-anchor prime.

## 3. Abstract cross-scale synchronization

Assume that for every `p in A` and every nonzero `d mod p`,

\[
 \sum_{q\in B}
 \left\|\frac{d q^{-1}}p\right\|^2
 \ge D_B.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

For the complete top prime block, PNT plus Brun--Titchmarsh gives

\[
 D_B\gg\frac Z{\log Z}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

For any circle points `x,y`,

\[
 \|x+y\|^2\ge\frac12\|x\|^2-\|y\|^2.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Hence, whenever `p` does not divide `Delta`, (2.1) gives

\[
 \sum_{q\in B}
 \left\|\frac{n_{p,q}(u,v)}{pq}\right\|^2
 \ge
 \frac{D_B}{2}
 -\frac{v^2}{p^2}\sum_{q\in B}\frac1{q^2}.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Since

\[
 \sum_{q\in B}\frac1{q^2}\asymp\frac1{Z\log Z}
\]

and `p^2 asymp Z`, the perturbation is

\[
 O\left(\frac{v^2}{Z^2\log Z}\right).
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Thus for every

\[
 |v|=o(Z^{3/2})
 \tag{3.6}
\]

one has, for large `Z`,

\[
 \sum_{q\in B}
 \left\|\frac{n_{p,q}(u,v)}{pq}\right\|^2
 \ge\frac{D_B}{4}
 \gg\frac Z{\log Z}.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 -- cross-scale label synchronization

Suppose

\[
 0<|u-v|<\prod_{p\in A}p
 \tag{3.8}
\]

and `|v|=o(Z^(3/2))`.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{p\in A}\sum_{q\in B}
 \left\|\frac{n_{p,q}(u,v)}{pq}\right\|^2
 \gg\frac Z{\log Z}.
 }
 \tag{3.9}
\]

#### Proof

If every `p in A` divided `u-v`, then their product would divide `u-v`,
contradicting (3.8).  Choose one `p` not dividing the difference and apply
(3.7).  `square`

### Robustness

Deleting a fixed number of exceptional primes from either anchor changes the
right side of (3.9) by at most `O(1)` per deleted top prime and does not affect
the order `Z/log Z`.

The label ranges generated by the local low-energy SBEE decomposition are far
below the primorial `prod_{p in A}p=exp(Theta(X))`, so condition (3.8) reduces to
`u != v`.  Inside a growing local main arc they also satisfy (3.6).

## 4. Sparse elimination of every non-anchor coordinate

Let `P_{<Z}` be the primes below `Z`.  For every prime

\[
 r\in P_{<Z}\setminus(A\cup B),
\]

the complete top block satisfies the weaker but uniform population bound

\[
 \frac1{|B|}\sum_{q\in B}
 \left\|\frac{d q^{-1}}r\right\|^2
 \gg\frac1{(\log Z)^2}
 \tag{4.1}
\]

for every nonzero `d mod r`.

This follows from the multiplicity lemma: `|B| asymp Z/log Z`, while one residue
class modulo `r<=Z/2` occurs at most `O(Z/r)` times in the interval `(Z/2,Z)`.

Sampling without replacement and a union bound over all pairs `(r,d)` yields one
actual-prime subset

\[
 T\subset B,
 \qquad
 |T|=O((\log Z)^3),
 \tag{4.2}
\]

such that

\[
 \min_{d\ne0\bmod r}
 \sum_{q\in T}
 \left\|\frac{d q^{-1}}r\right\|^2
 \gg\log Z
 \tag{4.3}
\]

simultaneously for every non-anchor prefix prime `r`.

Use sensor edges

\[
 E_{\rm sens}
 =\{rq:r\notin A\cup B,\ q\in T\}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Conditioned on the top-anchor assignment, arbitrary-shift product-fibre
compression eliminates every non-anchor coordinate with total cost `1+o(1)`.

The sensor loads are

\[
 \Lambda_{\rm sens}
 =O\left(\frac{(\log Z)^3\log\log Z}{Z}\right),
 \tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
 W_{2,{\rm sens}}
 =O\left(\frac{(\log Z)^3}{Z^2}\right),
 \tag{4.6}
\]

both negligible relative to (1.2)--(1.3).

## 5. The two-anchor coherent skeleton

Suppose the lowest and top anchor assignments lie in their local coherent
sectors with labels `u` and `v`.

- If `u != v`, Theorem 3.1 gives cross energy `Omega(Z/log Z)`, far above every
  entropy or variance scale in the argument.
- If `u=v=m`, every anchor-control edge has phase `m/e`, and therefore

\[
 Q_{\rm anch}(m)=m^2\sigma_{\rm anch}^2
 \tag{5.1}
\]

throughout the centered coherent range.

The sparse sensors decode every eliminated coordinate to `m mod r` uniformly
for

\[
 |m|\le D_0\sqrt{\log k}/\sigma_{\rm anch},
 \tag{5.2}
\]

because this is `O(sqrt(Z) poly(k))`, whereas the distinguished sensor energy is
`o(log Z)` even at the smallest prime.

Thus the entire prefix Fourier space collapses to one common integer label.
