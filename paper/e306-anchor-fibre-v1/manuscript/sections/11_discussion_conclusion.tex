\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:scope}

Erd\H{o}s Problem~306 is the historical point of entry, but the completed result is more naturally read as a theorem about reciprocal representation from one restricted denominator system.  For squarefree semiprimes the article determines exactly which positive rationals are represented, proves finite-deletion stability, constructs countably infinite proper refinement chains, and gives a sharp parameterized fixed-target profile.  The characterization now follows directly from the fixed-target theorem by prime dilution, without numerator induction or special reduced-denominator cases.

\subsection{What the parameter reveals}

The exponent $\gamma$ controls three distinct quantities.  The reciprocal load is $(\log\gamma)^2/2$, the candidate family has size asymptotic to $Z^2/(2\log^2Z)$, and the leading variance provider changes at $\gamma=2$.  The complete statement is the single total-variance formula
\[
 \sigma_{\mathcal E}^2
 \sim\alpha_{t,\gamma}(1-\alpha_{t,\gamma})
 \left(
  \frac1{2X^2\log^2X}
  +\frac{\tau(b)}{Z\log Z}
 \right),
 \qquad
 \tau(b)=\sum_{r\in S_b}\frac1{r^2}.
\]
When $\gamma>2$, complete prime pairs determine the leading term.  At $\gamma=2$, the finitely many target-coordinate rows dominate by a logarithmic factor, and for $1<\gamma<2$ they dominate by a power.  This phase transition no longer divides the proof architecture: the universal cutoff $T_0=\kappa_0\min(X^2,Z)$ and the prime-only transition sector give one exhaustive six-sector partition for every fixed $\gamma>1$.

The direct numerator $a$ has a much simpler role.  It changes the target character from $\exp(-2\pi ih/b)$ to $\exp(-2\pi iah/b)$ and changes the centring equation to $\theta\Lambda=a/b$.  The target-coordinate rows still depend only on the prime-divisor set $S_b$.  Once the full coordinates have been identified in the major and first minor ranges, exact centring cancels the linear phase.  In the transition and adaptive ranges only prime coordinates are used, so no hidden numerator-dependent decoder is required.

The entropy optimization has a genuine boundary distinction.  For targets below $1/2$, the balanced Bernoulli point is accessible and the maximum $\log2$ is attained.  At and above $1/2$, the best value lies at the excluded no-wrap boundary $\lambda_\gamma=1$, so only a supremum remains.  This distinction persists even though the coefficient theorem itself is valid throughout the open admissible region.

\subsection{Replacement, refinement and transfer}

There are two distinct replacement statements.  Replacing one denominator by an avoiding representation of its reciprocal is a local refinement and makes only that block strict.  Replacing every denominator simultaneously by pairwise disjoint supports avoiding the original representation gives a globally proper refinement.  The latter is the mechanism iterated in the countably infinite chain; the row--column theorem remains necessary for common refinements of different representations.

Finite avoidance has an immediate countable consequence.  At each stage only finitely many supports have been used, so the next finite level may avoid their union.  Simultaneous proper replacement then makes the next level proper, and forbidding all earlier levels makes the level supports pairwise disjoint.  No compactness, choice principle beyond ordinary finite choices, or transfinite recursion is needed.

The optimized direct target also sharpens the global transfer.  Every admissible rational contains a sufficiently small reciprocal $1/p$ whose subtraction preserves a squarefree reduced denominator.  The direct target $1/p$ lies below $1/2$, so its exponent may be chosen at the attained entropy maximum.  A fixed residual support then costs only $O(1)$ terms and leaves the leading count and Hamming scale unchanged.  The same replacement mechanism gives quantitatively many globally proper refinements after fixed blocks are chosen for all but one original term.

\subsection{Effectivity}

The argument is qualitative-effective in principle: once the constants in the prime number theorem estimates, compact Taylor bounds, cyclic row energy and sector inequalities are made explicit, the finite search takes place in a computable family.  The article does not supply a useful numerical threshold for $X$, nor explicit effective error terms in the sharp coefficient asymptotic.  Producing those quantities would require separate constant tracking and numerical proof engineering; it is not implicit in the asymptotic notation used here.

\subsection{Conclusion}

The proof shows why squarefreeness is sufficient, not merely that it is.  The CRT coordinates expose an anchor block which synchronizes local residues, anchor-cross rows recover every target direction, and weighted fibre compression preserves the prime-pair factors needed beyond the central range.  Parameterizing the outer prime scale reveals the exact load region, the variance boundary at $\gamma=2$, and the fixed-target entropy profile, while prime dilution converts the direct theorem into the full characterization.  The resulting positive coefficient yields exact equality, a sharp Gaussian asymptotic, entropy-optimal exponential multiplicity, and quantitatively branching proper refinement.  Thus a positive rational is representable by reciprocals of distinct squarefree semiprimes exactly when its reduced denominator is squarefree, and every such representation fibre is both directed and quantitatively abundant.
