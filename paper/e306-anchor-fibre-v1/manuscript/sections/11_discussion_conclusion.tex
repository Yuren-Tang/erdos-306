\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:scope}

The characterization and finite-avoidance theorem are global, whereas the quantitative analysis is tied to the explicit one-anchor family.  Its natural parameter range is the open centred no-wrap range $t<\lambda_\gamma<1$; the argument makes no impossibility claim beyond that range.

\subsection{Mechanism and scope}

The exponent $\gamma$ controls the reciprocal load, the size of the candidate family, and the change in the dominant variance contribution at $\gamma=2$.  The complete variance statement is
\[
 \sigma_{\mathcal E}^2
 \sim\alpha_{t,\gamma}(1-\alpha_{t,\gamma})
 \left(
  \frac1{2X^2\log^2X}
  +\frac{\tau(b)}{Z\log Z}
 \right),
 \qquad
 \tau(b)=\sum_{r\in S_b}\frac1{r^2}.
\]
The universal cutoff $T_0=\kappa_0\min(X^2,Z)$ and the prime-only transition sector allow the same six-sector proof on both sides of that variance boundary.  Apart from its scalar effect through $\theta=t/\Lambda$, the numerator $a$ enters the Fourier geometry only through the target character; the target-row system depends only on $b$.

The same distinction governs the counting statements.  The entropy optimization is exact within the direct family: below $1/2$ the balanced Bernoulli point is attained, while at and above $1/2$ only a boundary supremum occurs.  These statements do not assert global optimality among all squarefree-semiprime representations of a given height.  Likewise, local replacement makes one block strict, whereas simultaneous pairwise-disjoint replacement is what produces globally proper refinements; the transportation argument is needed to align different decompositions.
