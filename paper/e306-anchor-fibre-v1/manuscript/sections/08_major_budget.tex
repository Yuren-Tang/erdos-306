\section{The major contribution and terminal budget}
\label{sec:major-budget}

We now estimate Sectors I and II and assemble all five sectors.  The order of choices is essential: the Gaussian cutoff $C$ is fixed first, and only then is $X$ taken sufficiently large.  All constants introduced in the preceding sections are fixed once $b$ and the stated fixed numerical choices are fixed; none depends on $C$, and only the final threshold for $X$ may depend on $C$.

\subsection{A uniform local expansion}

Compactness of the Bernoulli interval $I_b$ gives constants $\rho_b>0$ and $M_b<\infty$ such that, uniformly for $\theta\in I_b$ and $|z|\leq\rho_b$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-log-expansion}
 \log\bigl((1-\theta)+\theta\exp(2\pi iz)\bigr)
 =2\pi i\theta z
 -2\pi^2\theta(1-\theta)z^2
 +R_\theta(z),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-log-remainder}
 |R_\theta(z)|\leq M_b|z|^3.
\end{equation}
The logarithm is taken on the branch through $1$; the compactness and small disk ensure that this branch is uniform in $\theta$.

\subsection{Sector I: positive real major}

For $|m|\leq N$, the decoded tuple is the genuine frequency $h=m\pmod L$.  Every denominator in $\mathcal E$ is at least $X^2$ for large $X$, while $N\asymp_C\sigma_{\mathcal E}^{-1}\asymp_b X\log X$.  Hence, uniformly for $|m|\leq N$,
\[
 \max_{e\in\mathcal E}|m/e|
 \ll_C\frac{\log X}{X}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aggregate-cubic-remainder}
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}|m/e|^3
 &\leq\frac{|m|^3}{X^2}
       \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac1{e^2}\\
 &\ll_b\frac{C^3}{\sigma_{\mathcal E}X^2}
 \ll_b C^3\frac{\log X}{X}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The remainder bound \cref{eq:local-log-remainder} shows that \cref{eq:local-log-expansion} may be summed over the actual denominator family, and the aggregate remainder is $o_{b,C}(1)$ uniformly in the major range.

The linear term cancels exactly by \cref{eq:exact-centering-load}:
\[
 2\pi i m\theta\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac1e
 -2\pi i m/b=0.
\]
The quadratic term is exactly the actual-family variance.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:major-local-gaussian}
 F(m)=
 \exp\bigl(-2\pi^2m^2\sigma_{\mathcal E}^2+\varepsilon_m\bigr),
 \qquad
 \max_{|m|\leq N}|\varepsilon_m|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The approximation \cref{eq:major-local-gaussian} shows that for sufficiently large $X$ the remainder is small enough that
\[
 \RePart F(m)
 \geq a_b\exp(-2\pi^2m^2\sigma_{\mathcal E}^2)
\]
with $a_b>0$ fixed.  The terms at $m$ and $-m$ are conjugate, so the major sum is real.  The interval
\[
 |m|\leq\frac1{4\sigma_{\mathcal E}}
\]
is contained in $|m|\leq N$ for fixed $C\geq1$; on it the Gaussian is bounded below and there are $\gg\sigma_{\mathcal E}^{-1}$ integers.  We have proved the following lower bound.

\begin{proposition}[Positive Gaussian major]
\label{prop:positive-major}
For every fixed $C\geq1$ and all sufficiently large $X$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-major}
 \RePart\sum_{|m|\leq N}F(m)
 \geq\frac{c_{\mathrm{maj}}}{\sigma_{\mathcal E}}
\end{equation}
for a constant $c_{\mathrm{maj}}=c_{\mathrm{maj}}(b)>0$ independent of $C$ and $X$.
\end{proposition}

The lower bound is \cref{eq:positive-major}.  This is a complex positive-real estimate.  It is not obtained by lower-bounding the moduli of individual Fourier factors.

\subsection{Sector II: the full-variance Gaussian tail}

For
\[
 N<|m|\leq X^2/4,
\]
the decoded frequency is again the genuine integer $m\pmod L$.  Since every $e\in\mathcal E$ is at least $X^2$,
\[
 |m|/e\leq1/4,
 \qquad
 \dist{m/e}=|m|/e.
\]
The global modulus bound \cref{eq:global-Fourier-energy} and the definition of $\sigma_{\mathcal E}$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:full-variance-gaussian}
 |F(m)|\leq\exp(-c_bm^2\sigma_{\mathcal E}^2)
\end{equation}
for a fixed $c_b>0$.  Since $N+1>C/\sigma_{\mathcal E}$, monotonicity and a Gaussian integral comparison give, for fixed $C\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sector-II-tail}
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{|m|>N}\exp(-c_bm^2\sigma_{\mathcal E}^2)
 &\leq 2\exp\bigl(-c_b(N+1)^2\sigma_{\mathcal E}^2\bigr)
      +2\int_{N+1}^{\infty}
        \exp(-c_bt^2\sigma_{\mathcal E}^2)\,dt\\
 &\leq K_b\sigma_{\mathcal E}^{-1}
       \exp(-c_bC^2/2),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $K_b$ is independent of $C$ and $X$; here $C$ is fixed before $X$.
By \cref{eq:sector-II-tail}, choose $C$ so large that the right side is less than
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:C-choice}
 \frac{c_{\mathrm{maj}}}{8\sigma_{\mathcal E}}.
\end{equation}
This is the only terminal constant chosen before $X$.

\subsection{Strict assembly}

We now fix the $C$ selected in \cref{eq:C-choice}.  Then choose $X$ above the finite maximum of the thresholds required for:
\begin{itemize}
\item the prime counts and Abel summations of \cref{sec:denominator-system};
\item separation from the support of $b$ and avoidance of $\mathcal T$;
\item the load interval, variance comparison and no-wrap inequality;
\item reciprocal dispersion, nondominant forcing, exact anchor rigidity and fingerprint entropy;
\item the weighted anchor partition;
\item both row-distance estimates and the row-tail sum;
\item both decoder-identification ranges;
\item containment and prime supply in the adaptive interval;
\item the Taylor disk and the aggregate remainder in \cref{eq:aggregate-cubic-remainder};
\item the strict $o(\sigma_{\mathcal E}^{-1})$ estimates in \cref{eq:sector-III-total,eq:sector-IV-total,eq:sector-V-total}.
\end{itemize}
This order is denoted
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:parameter-order}
 C\longrightarrow X.
\end{equation}
No constant depending on $C$ is subsequently treated as absolute after the order \cref{eq:parameter-order} is fixed.

For this choice of $X$, Sector II contributes at most $c_{\mathrm{maj}}/(8\sigma_{\mathcal E})$, and Sectors III--V together contribute less than, say, $3c_{\mathrm{maj}}/(8\sigma_{\mathcal E})$.  By \cref{prop:positive-major}, Sector I has real part at least $c_{\mathrm{maj}}/\sigma_{\mathcal E}$.  Applying \cref{prop:decoded-skeleton-positivity} to the five-sector partition gives the final analytic theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Positive Fourier numerator]
\label{thm:positive-fourier-numerator}
For every fixed squarefree $b\geq3$ and finite forbidden set $\mathcal T$, there exists a sufficiently large $X$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-full-sum}
 \RePart\sum_{h\bmod L}F(h)>0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof has used the exact weighted product error rather than a union bound over anchor assignments, retained every lower--lower factor needed in Sector III, and required target-coordinate decoding only in the ranges in which those coordinates enter the estimate.  The remaining work is no longer analytic: \cref{sec:exact-completion} turns this positive coefficient into an exact unit-fraction identity and completes the elementary reductions.
