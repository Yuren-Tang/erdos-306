\section{Further questions and perspective}\label{sec:scope}

The characterization in Theorem \ref{thm:characterization} applies to every positive rational with squarefree reduced denominator. The quantitative theorems have a narrower scope: they concern the single direct family attached to a fixed squarefree denominator $b\ge3$.

\subsection{Quantitative boundaries}

The elementary closure for denominators $1$ and $2$ does not produce one common family $\mathcal E_X$, one Bernoulli parameter, or one cardinality scale to which Theorems \ref{thm:local-profile} and \ref{thm:multiplicity} could be transferred. We make no quantitative claim for $b=1,2$.

The moving-target theorem is uniform on every fixed standardized compact interval
\[
\left|\frac{j}{L_X\sigma_X}\right|\le U.
\]
The threshold in $X$ may depend on $U$. We do not obtain a growing standardized radius $U=U_X\to\infty$, nor a gap-free theorem on a fixed target-independent primorial family. The real target window has radius $U\sigma_X\to0$; what grows is the number of lattice points inside that shrinking interval.

Likewise, Theorem \ref{thm:multiplicity} gives the ambient entropy exponent in a mesoscopic cardinality window and at least one exact cardinality. It does not give a prescribed-cardinality asymptotic, a conditional central limit theorem on the natural $\sqrt{M_X}$ scale, or a bivariate local limit for reciprocal sum and cardinality.

\subsection{Finite avoidance is not deletion resilience}

Finite avoidance allows the family to be chosen after a fixed finite forbidden set is named. It does not assert robustness of one already constructed family under a small adversarial deletion.

Fix a prime $r\mid b$ and delete the entire target row
\[
\mathcal D_{r,X}
=
\{rq:q\in\mathcal B_X\}.
\tag{10.1}
\]
Its size is $|\mathcal B_X|=o(M_X)$. After this deletion, every remaining integer weight $L_X/e$ is divisible by $r$, whereas
\[
\frac{L_X}{b}
=
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_X}p
\]
is nonzero modulo $r$. Thus no remaining subset can represent $1/b$, even modulo $r$.

The example clarifies the role of the target rows. They are negligible in reciprocal mass but load-bearing in observability. Any genuine resilience theorem would have to preserve these sensors together with enough anchor rigidity, variance, retained damping, lattice span, and no-wrap structure.

\subsection{A reusable finite-Fourier pattern}

The proof separates into four ingredients which are more general than the particular statement proved here:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a weighted anchor partition which turns low internal energy into a small family of coherent labels;
\item quantitative row observability which identifies the remaining CRT coordinates;
\item a compression of the decoded skeleton which sums off-decoder states without paying the raw fibre cardinality;
\item retained factors which suppress coherent labels after the decoder has done its work.
\end{enumerate}
Together with a positive Gaussian major term and a no-wrap condition, these ingredients suggest a broader finite Fourier transference principle. When low energy forces a small coherent skeleton, arithmetic rows observe the remaining coordinates, off-skeleton mass can be compressed without discarding the factors responsible for damping, and the zero-frequency model is Gaussian, exact subset-sum information may persist down to the natural lattice scale. The semiprime construction above supplies one concrete arithmetic realization of this pattern.

Two directions arise directly from the present boundaries. First, one may ask how much of the characterization and local fibre geometry survives when the allowed prime factors are restricted to structured positive-density sets. Second, one may seek finer statistics inside the exact fibre: growing standardized target windows, local asymptotics at prescribed cardinalities, or joint local laws for reciprocal sum and cardinality. Each requires arithmetic or probabilistic input beyond what is used here.

\subsection{Final perspective}

The squarefree denominator is both the elementary obstruction and the exact one. The converse is not proved by recursively splitting unit fractions. Instead we construct a large arithmetic family, use its prime factors as a finite system of sensors, and show that the exact target coefficient has the local form predicted by a Gaussian lattice heuristic.

Thus the resolution of Erd\H{o}s Problem 306 comes with a stronger phenomenon. For every fixed squarefree $b\ge3$, the direct family contains exponentially many representations of $1/b$; these representations occupy cardinality layers at the ambient entropy rate; and the same family realizes a growing consecutive block of nearby exact targets with a Gaussian profile. This local fibre geometry is the main reason to regard the Fourier construction as more than an existence argument.
