\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:scope}

The proof is tailored to reciprocal sums of semiprimes, yet several of its mechanisms do not depend on the particular prime blocks used above.  Their value lies less in a universal black-box theorem than in showing where information is preserved, where it is discarded, and which arithmetic input must replace it.

\subsection{Reusable mechanisms}

The exact weighted product-fibre theorem, \cref{thm:weighted-compression}, is a deterministic statement about finite product spaces.  It requires neither a CRT decomposition nor a probabilistic interpretation.  Its essential feature is the weighting: off-decoder mass is averaged against the actual anchor weight, rather than multiplied by the cardinality of the anchor-assignment space.  The tensorized expression is optimal from the displayed row data alone.  Equally important, every factor not assigned to a row remains on the decoded skeleton and may continue to provide cancellation or modulus decay.

The syndrome results, \cref{thm:syndrome-separation,prop:qualitative-observability}, give a finite-group language for target-coordinate sensitivity.  Their invariant is the pseudometric
\[
 d_\Psi(h,h')^2
 =\sum_j\dist{\psi_j(h-h')}^2.
\]
The zero-distance subgroup is exactly the common kernel of the sensors.  In the present argument, the anchor-cross rows are the sensors, and squarefreeness decomposes the otherwise unobserved target group into prime coordinates which can be detected separately.  Qualitative injectivity alone is not enough for an asymptotic application: the row estimates require a quantitative lower bound for the nonzero syndrome distance.

The decoded-skeleton proposition, \cref{prop:decoded-skeleton-positivity}, combines one major contribution, finitely many minor contributions and the product-fibre error without counting any of them twice.  It imposes no universal taxonomy of major, middle, outer or energetic frequencies; the arithmetic problem determines the sectors.  An application must construct the anchor partition, identify the decoders and retain factors that control what remains.

Finally, \cref{thm:complete-family-collision} separates complete-family mass from synchronization.  It applies to every nonnegative weighted complete $k$-uniform family and identifies the moving-uniformity range
\[
 k^2V/U^2\longrightarrow0
\]
in which the first two power sums determine the total mass asymptotically.  For the present pair family only the exact identity $e_2=(U^2-V)/2$ is used.  No rigidity or decoding principle follows from the collision estimate alone.

\subsection{Other product measures}

The Fourier product formula of \cref{lem:independent-product} extends immediately from Bernoulli selectors to independent finite-valued random variables: each Bernoulli factor is replaced by the Fourier transform of the corresponding local law.  Whenever the character space admits an anchor--fibre decomposition and those local transforms satisfy suitable decoder and residual estimates, the same weighted compression and skeleton assembly remain available.

The algebraic decomposition also makes sense for signed or complex local weights after magnitudes are assigned to the row kernels and all phases are retained in the residual factor.  The probabilistic conclusion must then be replaced by a statement about a nonzero or positive coefficient.  By contrast, a genuinely sequential kernel
\[
 a_r(x_r\mid x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1},y)
\]
does not have the product form used in \cref{thm:weighted-compression}; it would require a different conditional theorem.

\subsection{Where information is lost}

The normalized form \cref{cor:normalized-fibre} allows the anchor-dependent error
\[
 \sum_yA_y\left\{\prod_r(1+\varepsilon_{r,y})-1\right\}.
\]
Thus poorly decoded anchor assignments can be harmless when their decoded anchor mass is small.  The present proof uses uniform row tails followed by the global anchor partition, but the deterministic statement itself is genuinely averaged rather than worst-case.

Its principal limitation is also clear: the theorem takes absolute values on every off-decoder tuple.  It cannot exploit cancellation within one fibre or between different fibres.  A result retaining such complex cancellation would be a new mechanism, not merely an improvement in the row-distance constants.

Two further losses are easy to make accidentally.  First, replacing the weighted anchor sum by the raw number of anchor assignments can destroy a true estimate.  Second, bounding all unassigned factors by $1$ before the decoder is chosen may remove the only available damping in an intermediate frequency range.  Sector~III works precisely because the lower--lower factors remain visible after the rows have been compressed.

Target observability is a third, logically separate issue.  If a nontrivial target direction lies in the common kernel of all sensors and retained factors, its character may cancel an apparently positive contribution from the observed coordinates.  Every such direction must therefore be fixed by the anchor assignment, resolved by a decoder, or detected by a residual factor in each range where it matters.

\subsection{Possible extensions and present limits}

The one-anchor geometry is especially natural for pair denominators: one prime lies in the anchor block and the other indexes a row, while pairs disjoint from the anchor survive as residual factors.  For denominators with $k>2$ prime factors, the complete-family collision estimate still controls mass in its natural range, but it does not provide the higher-order synchronization or decoding theory that such a construction would need.

Further progress would require genuinely new information at one of the places where the present proof discards it: fibre estimates that preserve complex cancellation, synchronization principles that avoid low-energy fingerprint counting, or quantitative observability for target groups whose syndrome distance varies with scale.  A second arithmetic application would help distinguish the intrinsic parts of the architecture from those peculiar to this semiprime system.  For now, the structural tools are best read in the proof that requires them.

\subsection{Conclusion}

The proof shows why squarefreeness is sufficient, not merely that it is.  Squarefreeness gives a CRT coordinate system in which the anchor block synchronizes many local residues to one integer label and the anchor-cross rows recover the remaining prime and target coordinates.  Weighted fibre compression then preserves the lower--lower factors needed beyond the central range.  Together with the full variance and the anchor energy, those factors control every sector outside the positive Gaussian major contribution; the no-wrap step finally returns from the finite quotient to an exact rational identity.  The same positive coefficient supplies, for fixed squarefree $b\geq3$, an exponential family of exact representations with entropy-optimal cardinality growth.  Thus the condition forced by least common multiples also supplies the coordinate structure from which the representation is constructed.  A positive rational is a sum of reciprocals of distinct squarefree semiprimes exactly when its reduced denominator is squarefree.
