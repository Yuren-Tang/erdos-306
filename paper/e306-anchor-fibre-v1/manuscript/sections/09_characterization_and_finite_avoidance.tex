\section{Characterization and finite-avoidance consequences}\label{sec:characterization}

The analytic work is complete. We now pass from a direct proper target to arbitrary positive rationals and record the qualitative consequences of finite avoidance.

\subsection{The direct proper-target theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Avoiding representation of a proper target]\label{thm:avoiding-target}
Let $t=a/b\in(0,1)$ be reduced with squarefree $b$, let $T\subset\N$ be finite, and fix $\gamma>1$ with
\[
t<\frac{(\log\gamma)^2}{2}<1.
\]
Then, for all sufficiently large $X$, there is a finite set $A\subseteq\mathcal E_X$ of pairwise distinct squarefree semiprimes, disjoint from $T$, such that
\[
\sum_{e\in A}\frac1e=t.
\tag{9.1}\label{eq:9.1}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma \ref{lem:arithmetic-shape}, for sufficiently large $X$ the direct family consists of pairwise distinct squarefree semiprimes outside $T$. The central local limit \eqref{eq:6.28} gives $P=W_X(t)>0$ for all sufficiently large $X$, so some subset represents $t$ exactly.
\end{proof}

Every proper rational target is covered by some exponent in this regime. Indeed, for $0<t<1$ the interval
\[
e^{\sqrt{2t}}
<\gamma<e^{\sqrt2}
\tag{9.2}\label{eq:9.2}
\]
is nonempty.

\subsection{The squarefree-denominator characterization}

We can now prove Theorem \ref{thm:characterization} without numerator induction or special cases for reduced denominators $1$ and $2$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:characterization}]
For necessity, suppose
\[
q=\sum_{e\in A}\frac1e
\]
with every $e$ squarefree, and put $D=\operatorname{lcm}(A)$. Then
\[
q=\frac{\sum_{e\in A}D/e}{D}.
\]
The reduced denominator of $q$ divides the squarefree integer $D$, and is therefore squarefree.

Conversely, let
\[
q=\frac ab>0
\]
be reduced with squarefree $b$, and let $T$ be a prescribed finite forbidden set. Choose a prime $p>a$ with $p\nmid b$ and set
\[
t=\frac{a}{bp}.
\tag{9.3}\label{eq:9.3}
\]
Then $0<t<1$. Since $p>a$, one has $p\nmid a$; together with $(a,b)=1$ and $p\nmid b$, this shows that \eqref{eq:9.3} is reduced and has squarefree denominator $bp$.

Choose once and for all a $\gamma$ satisfying \eqref{eq:9.2} for this $t$. Construct supports $A_1,\ldots,A_p$ successively. At stage $j$, apply Theorem \ref{thm:avoiding-target} to $t$, forbidding
\[
T\cup A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_{j-1}.
\]
The supports are pairwise disjoint, each avoids $T$, and each has reciprocal sum $t$. Their union therefore represents
\[
pt=\frac ab=q.
\]
This proves sufficiency and arbitrary finite avoidance for every squarefree reduced denominator, including $1$ and $2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Thresholds, disjoint supports, and many terms}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:qualitative-abundance}
Let $q>0$ have squarefree reduced denominator.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For every $N$, $q$ has a representation all of whose denominators exceed $N$.
\item $q$ has infinitely many representations with pairwise disjoint supports.
\item $q$ has representations with arbitrarily many terms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For (i), forbid $\{1,\ldots,N\}$. For (ii), construct representations recursively, at each stage forbidding every denominator used earlier. For (iii), use (i): if every denominator exceeds $N$, then every unit fraction is less than $1/N$, so any representation of the fixed positive number $q$ has more than $qN$ terms once $N$ is large.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite prescription}

\begin{proposition}[Extension of a partial support]\label{prop:finite-prescription}
Let $q>0$ have squarefree reduced denominator. Let $S$ be a finite set of distinct squarefree semiprimes such that
\[
\sum_{e\in S}\frac1e\le q,
\tag{9.4}\label{eq:9.4}
\]
and let $T$ be a finite set disjoint from $S$. Then $S$ extends to a representation of $q$ by distinct squarefree semiprimes which avoids $T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If equality holds in \eqref{eq:9.4}, there is nothing to add. Otherwise set
\[
r=q-\sum_{e\in S}\frac1e>0.
\]
Write $q$ and all $1/e$, $e\in S$, over one common denominator $D$. Since the reduced denominator of $q$ and every $e\in S$ is squarefree, $D$ is squarefree. Thus $r=N/D$ for some positive integer $N$, and the reduced denominator of $r$ divides $D$. Theorem \ref{thm:characterization} therefore represents $r$ using denominators outside $S\cup T$. Adjoining that support to $S$ gives the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Common proper refinements]\label{rem:common-refinement}
Finite avoidance also gives common proper refinements. For two representations of the same $q$, take the squarefree least common multiple $D$ of all their denominators, write the resulting integer weights as row and column sums of a nonnegative integral transportation matrix, and represent each positive cell value $x_{ij}/D$ successively while forbidding every denominator already used. The resulting pairwise disjoint cell supports refine both representations; iteration handles any finite family and any additional finite forbidden set.
\end{remark}

These structural consequences are not used elsewhere; they show that finite avoidance is substantially stronger than bare existence.
