\section{Finite Fourier selection and exactness}
\label{sec:fourier-selection}

We record the finite-group selection identity used throughout the proof and separate quotient realization from exact equality in the ambient group.

\subsection{Character orthogonality}

Let $A$ be a finite abelian group and let $\widehat A$ be its character group.  We write characters multiplicatively.  For $a,t\in A$,
\[
 \frac1{|A|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A}\overline{\chi(t)}\chi(a)
 =\mathbf 1_{a=t}.
\]
Consequently, if $T$ is an $A$-valued random variable, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite-fourier-coefficient}
 \Prob(T=t)
 =\frac1{|A|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A}
   \overline{\chi(t)}\E\chi(T).
\end{equation}
The numerator on the right is the real nonnegative number $|A|\Prob(T=t)$.  Thus it is enough to prove that its real part is positive.

\begin{lemma}[Independent product formula]
\label{lem:independent-product}
Let $v_j\in A$, and let $\xi_j$ be independent Bernoulli random variables with parameters $\theta_j\in[0,1]$.  For
\[
 T=\sum_j\xi_jv_j
\]
and every $\chi\in\widehat A$,
\[
 \E\chi(T)=\prod_j\bigl((1-\theta_j)+\theta_j\chi(v_j)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\chi$ is a homomorphism,
\[
 \chi(T)=\prod_j\chi(v_j)^{\xi_j}.
\]
Independence gives the product of the expectations, and
\[
 \E\chi(v_j)^{\xi_j}
 =(1-\theta_j)+\theta_j\chi(v_j).
\]
\end{proof}

The endpoint parameters $0$ and $1$ cause no difficulty.  More generally, the same argument applies to independent finite-valued variables, with each Bernoulli factor replaced by the Fourier transform of the corresponding local law.

\subsection{Reciprocal subset sums}

Let $\mathcal E$ be a finite set of positive integers and let $L$ be divisible by every $e\in\mathcal E$.  Associate to $e$ the residue
\[
 v_e=\frac{L}{e}\pmod L
\]
in $A=\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z$.  If $\xi_e$ are independent Bernoulli variables with common parameter $\theta$, then
\[
 T=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\xi_ev_e
\]
encodes the subset reciprocal sum modulo $1$: the congruence $T=L/b\pmod L$ is equivalent to
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac{\xi_e}{e}
 \equiv \frac1b\pmod 1.
\]
Writing characters of $A$ as $h\mapsto \exp(2\pi i h\,\cdot/L)$, \cref{eq:finite-fourier-coefficient} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reciprocal-fourier}
 L\Prob\left(T=\frac{L}{b}\right)
 =\sum_{h\bmod L}
   \exp\left(-\frac{2\pi i h}{b}\right)
   \prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
   \left((1-\theta)+\theta\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i h}{e}\right)\right).
\end{equation}
We shall prove that the real part of the sum on the right is positive.

The linear phase is centred by choosing
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:theta-centering}
 \theta\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac1e=\frac1b.
\end{equation}
For the E306 family this is achieved by $\theta=1/(b\Lambda)$, where $\Lambda$ is its total reciprocal load.

\subsection{Quotient realization and ambient equality}

Fourier positivity in a quotient proves only a quotient target.  We therefore record the exactness step separately.

Let $M$ be an abelian group, let $\pi:M\to A$ be a homomorphism to a finite abelian group, let $T_M$ be an $M$-valued random variable, and let $t_M\in M$.  Positivity of the coefficient of $\pi(t_M)$ gives
\[
 \Prob\bigl(\pi(T_M)=\pi(t_M)\bigr)>0.
\]
This implies $\Prob(T_M=t_M)>0$ under any of the following conditions.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness interfaces]
\label{prop:exactness-interfaces}
Each of the following is sufficient.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There is a set $C\subset M$ containing $t_M$ and the support of $T_M$ such that $\pi|_C$ is injective.
\item The support of $T_M$ and $t_M$ lie in one fundamental domain for $\ker\pi$.
\item One has the strict measurable alias inequality
\[
 \Prob\bigl(\pi(T_M)=\pi(t_M)\bigr)
 >\Prob\bigl(T_M\in(t_M+\ker\pi)\setminus\{t_M\}\bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
If $M$ is discrete and the relevant fibre is countable, the probability in~(iii) may equivalently be written as a sum over the nonzero aliases.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under~(i) or~(ii), the only point in the relevant quotient fibre which can lie in the support is $t_M$.  For~(iii), decompose the quotient event disjointly as
\[
 \{T_M=t_M\}\sqcup
 \{T_M\in(t_M+\ker\pi)\setminus\{t_M\}\}.
\]
The strict inequality forces the first event to have positive probability.
\end{proof}

In the present construction, all reciprocal subset sums and the target lie in the discrete group
\[
 M=L^{-1}\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Q,
\]
and reduction modulo $\mathbb Z$ gives the finite quotient
\[
 A=M/\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z.
\]
We use only the fundamental-domain criterion.  For the fixed-denominator construction with $b\geq3$, the total reciprocal load is less than $1$, so every possible subset sum lies in $[0,1)$, as does the target $1/b$.  Hence congruence modulo $1$ is exact equality.  This no-wrap argument is invoked only after positivity has been proved.

\subsection{Chinese-remainder coordinates}

For the E306 construction the period $L$ is squarefree.  Hence the Chinese remainder theorem identifies
\[
 \mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z
 \cong \prod_{p\mid L}\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,
\]
and similarly for the character group.  This coordinate system reveals the anchor--fibre architecture.

A chosen block of large prime coordinates forms the anchor.  The remaining prime coordinates are grouped into rows whose kernels depend on the anchor assignment.  Denominator factors involving two anchor primes contribute to the anchor weight; factors involving one anchor prime and one row prime contribute to that row; every other factor remains in a residual term on the decoded skeleton.  This is an exact partition of the original Fourier product, not an inequality or a heuristic decomposition.

The structural results of the next section explain how a product over these coordinates can be compressed to one coherent skeleton and then assembled into a positive coefficient.  The arithmetic sections verify the required anchor partition, row separation, decoder identification and skeleton damping for the concrete prime family.
