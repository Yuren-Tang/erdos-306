\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

An integer is a \emph{squarefree semiprime} if it is the product of two distinct primes.  We resolve the reciprocal-representation question posed by Erd\H{o}s and Graham~\cite{erdos-graham-1980} and now recorded as Erd\H{o}s Problem~306~\cite{erdos-problem-306}.

\begin{theorem}[Squarefree-denominator characterization with finite avoidance]
\label{thm:main}
Let $q>0$ be rational.  The denominator of $q$ in lowest terms is squarefree if and only if, for every finite set $\mathcal T$ of positive integers, there is a finite set $\mathcal N$ of pairwise distinct squarefree semiprimes, disjoint from $\mathcal T$, such that
\[
 q=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\frac1n.
\]
\end{theorem}

The obstruction is elementary: a finite sum of reciprocals of squarefree integers has squarefree reduced denominator.  The content of \cref{thm:main} is the sufficiency direction, strengthened by finite avoidance.  The same result gives finite prescription, disjoint realization, common refinements, and countably infinite proper refinement chains.

\subsection{Context and comparison}

Egyptian-fraction representations and their restricted-denominator variants have a long history; see Bloom and Elsholtz~\cite{bloom-elsholtz-2022} for a recent survey.  Barbeau gave the first known representation of $1$ by reciprocals of distinct squarefree semiprimes, using $101$ terms; Watanabe later found the currently shortest known examples, with $47$ terms~\cite{barbeau-1977,watanabe-2020}.  Butler, Erd\H{o}s and Graham proved that every positive integer is a sum of distinct unit fractions whose denominators have three distinct prime factors, and conjectured the corresponding statement with two prime factors~\cite{butler-erdos-graham-2015}.

A recent preprint of Li proves the two-prime conjecture for every positive integer.  For a general squarefree denominator $b$, it also represents $a/b$ above an explicit threshold and leaves the remaining below-threshold $\omega=2$ regime open~\cite{li-2026-semiprime}.  The theorem above settles that remaining rational case.  Li's proof uses additive-combinatorial subset-sum input and induction.  Here a fixed target is obtained directly from a positive finite Fourier coefficient; prime dilution on disjoint supports then gives the global characterization.

Quantitative multiplicity is known in the unrestricted setting on its natural ambient scale.  Conlon, Fox, He, Mubayi, Pham, Suk and Verstra\"ete prove that, for every fixed positive rational $q$, there are $2^{(c_q+o(1))N}$ representations by distinct denominators at most $N$~\cite{conlon-et-al-2025}.  Our quantitative statements concern the much thinner squarefree-semiprime system and are measured by the size of the explicit family constructed below.

An earlier Lean~4 formalization established the original Erd\H{o}s 306 existence statement~\cite{tang-erdos-306-formal}.  The present article proves a broader theorem through a substantially reorganized analytic argument.

\subsection{The mechanism and its interfaces}

The proof is organized around one continuous Fourier--CRT mechanism.  The exact coefficient identity is separated from the estimates used to prove positivity.  In the compact composition theorem of \cref{thm:one-well-composition}, finite Fourier semantics, an admissible observation/compression provider, the actual local kernel, a source-uniform weighted tail estimate and exact realization are distinct inputs.  The reciprocal construction supplies each input concretely.

A block of the largest primes acts as an anchor: sufficiently small internal energy forces all anchor coordinates to be residues of one integer label $m$.  The factors joining an anchor prime to a remaining prime form anchor-cross rows.  Their cyclic energy identifies the remaining prime coordinates; analogous rows indexed by the prime divisors of the reduced target denominator observe the target coordinates.

The row estimates alone cannot be multiplied over the raw set of anchor assignments.  The exact weighted fibre identity of \cref{thm:weighted-compression} performs the required compression: above each anchor assignment it retains one decoded tuple and bounds the full off-decoder mass without dividing by decoder weights.  Every factor not assigned to a row remains on the decoded skeleton.  This preservation is essential.  The lower--lower prime-pair factors survive the compression and provide the Gaussian transition and adaptive damping which control frequencies beyond the full-coordinate range.

The central range then has the expected Gaussian form.  Exact centring cancels the linear phase, the full family supplies the quadratic variance, and the aggregate cubic error is negligible.  The resulting positive quotient coefficient is converted to exact equality only afterwards: all subset sums and the target lie in one interval of length less than one.  Finally, prime dilution, finite avoidance and replacement transfer the fixed-target theorem to arbitrary positive rationals with squarefree reduced denominator.

The prime number theorem is the sole non-elementary number-theoretic input; a standard reference is Montgomery and Vaughan~\cite[Chapter~1]{montgomery-vaughan-2007}.  We use it for prime counts, Chebyshev's function, reciprocal prime mass and prime square load.  The anchor rigidity, row separation, fibre compression, target observability and Fourier-sector estimates are proved here.

\subsection{The direct fixed-family theorem}

Fix a reduced rational
\[
 t=\frac ab\in(0,1),
\]
with squarefree $b$, and fix $\gamma>1$.  Since $t$ is positive and less than one, reducedness entails $b>1$.  At scale $X$ put $Z=X^\gamma$, take primes in $[X,Z)$, and designate the upper fixed-ratio block $[Z/2,Z)$ as the anchor.  The denominator family consists of all unordered products of two distinct primes in $[X,Z)$, together with rows $rq$ for $r\mid b$ prime and $q$ in the anchor block.  Its reciprocal load tends to
\[
 \lambda_\gamma=\frac{(\log\gamma)^2}{2}.
\]
The centred no-wrap construction applies in the exact range
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intro-admissible-region}
 t<\lambda_\gamma<1,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 \exp(\sqrt{2t})<\gamma<\exp(\sqrt2).
\end{equation}
Exact centring chooses $\theta\Lambda=t$, so
\[
 \theta\longrightarrow\alpha_{t,\gamma}
 =\frac{t}{\lambda_\gamma}
 =\frac{2t}{(\log\gamma)^2}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Parameterized direct fixed-target theorem]
\label{thm:parameterized-direct-target-intro}
Let $t=a/b\in(0,1)$ be reduced with squarefree $b$, let $\gamma$ satisfy \cref{eq:intro-admissible-region}, and let $\mathcal T$ be finite.  For all sufficiently large $X$, the parameterized family of \cref{sec:denominator-system} contains a subset $\mathcal A$, disjoint from $\mathcal T$, such that
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal A}\frac1e=t.
\]
Its exact target coefficient satisfies the sharp Gaussian asymptotic of \cref{thm:sharp-target-coefficient}; the same fixed family satisfies the compact-window moving-target theorem \cref{thm:compact-moving-target}.
\end{theorem}

Put
\[
 \tau(b)=\sum_{\substack{r\mid b\\r\ \mathrm{prime}}}\frac1{r^2}>0.
\]
The variance of the actual Bernoulli family has the single asymptotic
\[
 \sigma_{\mathcal E}^2
 \sim\alpha_{t,\gamma}(1-\alpha_{t,\gamma})
 \left(
  \frac1{2X^2\log^2X}
  +\frac{\tau(b)}{Z\log Z}
 \right).
\]
At $\gamma=2$ the dominant contribution changes: prime pairs dominate for $\gamma>2$, while the target rows dominate for $1<\gamma\leq2$.  The proof itself remains uniform.  A full-coordinate cutoff
\[
 T_0=\kappa_0\min(X^2,Z)
\]
is followed by a prime-only Gaussian transition and an adaptive retained-pair range, giving one pairwise-disjoint six-sector decomposition for every fixed $\gamma>1$.

\subsection{The coefficient and its consequences}

The fixed-target coefficient satisfies
\[
 L_XP_X(t)\sim\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_X}.
\]
More generally, for each fixed $U>0$, the same family has the uniform Gaussian profile of \cref{thm:compact-moving-target} for integers $|j|\leq U L_X\sigma_X$ while the shifted target remains in the exact load and no-wrap range.  No growing standardized window is claimed.

The representations in the direct family have exponential rate
\[
 H(\alpha_{t,\gamma})
 =-\alpha_{t,\gamma}\log\alpha_{t,\gamma}
  -(1-\alpha_{t,\gamma})\log(1-\alpha_{t,\gamma}).
\]
For $0<t<1/2$, the maximum $\log2$ is attained uniquely at $\gamma=\exp(2\sqrt t)$.  At $t=1/2$ it is a non-attained supremum, and for $1/2<t<1$ the non-attained supremum is $H(t)$.  The same coefficient yields exact-cardinality extraction, exponentially large Hamming-separated families, balanced signed reciprocal relations and denominator-height estimates.  Finite replacement and prime dilution then give quantitative proper-refinement branching and transfer to every admissible rational.

\subsection{Conventions and organization}

The terms \emph{anchor assignment}, \emph{coherent label}, \emph{row decoder}, \emph{fibre} and \emph{decoded skeleton} refer respectively to the CRT coordinates on the anchor block, the synchronizing integer $m$, the minimizing residue in one anchor-cross row, the product of all row coordinates over a fixed anchor assignment, and the single decoded tuple together with every unassigned factor.  The integer label $m$ and the decoded residue class $h_m\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z$ are distinct objects; they coincide as a full CRT tuple only in the full-coordinate range.

Throughout the direct construction, $t$, $\gamma$, $b$ and the finite forbidden set are fixed while $X\to\infty$.  The constants $\kappa_{\rm mod}=\kappa_{\rm mod}(t,\gamma)$, $c_{\rm row}=c_{\rm row}(t,\gamma,b)$ and $\kappa_0=\kappa_0(t,\gamma,b)$ have distinct roles.  Other unnamed constants may depend on the fixed tuple and fixed numerical choices, but not on $X$ or on the later Gaussian cutoff $C$.  Whenever $C$ occurs, $X\to\infty$ is taken with $C$ fixed, and only afterwards may $C\to\infty$.
