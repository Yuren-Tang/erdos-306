\begin{abstract}
We prove that a positive rational number is a finite sum of reciprocals of distinct integers, each the product of two distinct primes, if and only if its reduced denominator is squarefree; moreover, the representation may avoid any prescribed finite set of denominators.  This finite-avoidance theorem yields finite prescription, disjoint realization of finite decompositions, and common refinements of arbitrary finite families of representations.  For the analytic core, we choose a complete family of semiprime denominators and express the subset-sum problem as a finite Fourier coefficient.  A block of large primes synchronizes the relevant local residues to one integer frequency, while weighted fibre compression preserves the unused prime-pair factors needed to control the complementary ranges.  The positive coefficient gives exact equality by a no-wrap argument.  For each fixed squarefree $b\geq3$, it also has a sharp fixed-target asymptotic and yields exponential multiplicity at the optimal entropy rate for the constructed mesoscopic cardinality window, including an exponentially large equal-cardinality family whose supports are macroscopically separated.  Denominator-height bounds and a finite residual transfer extend this quantitative abundance to every positive rational with squarefree reduced denominator.
\end{abstract}
