\begin{abstract}
We prove that a positive rational number is a finite sum of reciprocals of distinct integers, each the product of two distinct primes, if and only if its reduced denominator is squarefree.  For the converse, it is enough to represent $1/b$ for each squarefree $b$ while avoiding finitely many denominators.  We choose a complete family of semiprime denominators and express the subset-sum problem as a finite Fourier coefficient.  A block of large primes forces the relevant local residues to arise from one integer frequency, while rows across that block determine the remaining coordinates.  A weighted compression estimate preserves the unused prime-pair factors, whose decay controls intermediate frequencies.  The Gaussian contribution near zero then dominates the four complementary sectors.  Character orthogonality produces the required congruence modulo one, and the fact that the total reciprocal mass is less than one makes this congruence an exact equality.  For each fixed squarefree $b\geq3$, the same coefficient yields exponentially many exact representations, with the entropy-optimal exponential rate in a mesoscopic cardinality window and at one exact cardinality.  Avoidance and induction on the numerator complete the characterization.
\end{abstract}
