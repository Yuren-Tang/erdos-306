\title{Distinct Semiprime Egyptian Fractions for Rationals with Squarefree Denominator}
\author{Yuren Tang}
\date{Manuscript v1, based on software release v0.0.3}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We prove that every positive rational number whose reduced denominator is
squarefree is a finite sum of distinct unit fractions with denominators equal
to products of two distinct primes.  The proof is constructive in the
existential sense and is formalized in Lean~4 and Mathlib.  Its analytic core
is a finite circle-method argument for a weighted subset-sum problem.  A
dyadic system of prime blocks supplies three families of squarefree
semiprimes: control edges for the minor arcs, a residual mass batch, and
denominator-sensitive gadget edges.  The squarefree hypothesis is used both
as the necessary local obstruction and, in the sufficiency proof, to promote
congruence modulo every prime divisor of the denominator to congruence modulo
the denominator itself.  Two explicit estimates of Rosser and Schoenfeld are
the only nonstandard analytic inputs accepted by the released Lean proof:
a lower bound for primes in a dyadic interval and two-sided bounds for the
sum of reciprocals of primes.  We give a human-readable reconstruction of the
released proof, state its exact trust boundary, and record the relation
between this article, software release v0.0.3, and its archived DOI.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and the result}

An integer will be called a \emph{squarefree semiprime} if it has the form
\(pq\), where \(p<q\) are primes.  Thus both the number \(\omega(n)\) of
distinct prime divisors and the number \(\Omega(n)\) of prime factors counted
with multiplicity are equal to \(2\).

The problem considered here asks which positive rational numbers admit an
Egyptian-fraction expansion
\[
        q=\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n
\]
in which \(S\) is a finite set of squarefree semiprimes.  Because \(S\) is a
set, the denominators are distinct.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let \(q>0\) be rational and write \(q=a/b\) in lowest terms with \(b>0\).
If \(b\) is squarefree, then there are distinct squarefree semiprimes
\(n_1<\cdots<n_k\) such that
\[
             q=\frac1{n_1}+\cdots+\frac1{n_k}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The squarefree condition is also necessary.  Indeed, if every denominator
\(n\in S\) is squarefree, then the denominator of
\(\sum_{n\in S}1/n\), after reduction, divides
\(\lcm(S)\), hence is squarefree.  Consequently Theorem~\ref{thm:main},
together with this elementary observation, gives the expected
characterization.

The released formal theorem is stated in a deliberately rigid finite-sequence
encoding.  It asserts that for every positive \(q\in\Q\) with squarefree
Lean denominator there exist \(k\in\N\) and a strictly increasing map
\[
       n:\operatorname{Fin}(k+1)\longrightarrow\N
\]
with \(n(0)=1\), such that for every index \(i\) from \(1\) through the final
index one has
\(\omega(n(i))=\Omega(n(i))=2\), and
\[
       q=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac1{n(i)}.
\]
The initial value \(n(0)=1\) is a dummy anchor.  Strict monotonicity then
places every actual denominator above \(1\), and the interval of indices
\(1,\ldots,k\) records the nonempty finite expansion.

\section{Formal encoding and proof organization}

The formal development uses the predicate
\[
 \operatorname{IsSemiprime}(n)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\,p<q\text{ prime},\ n=pq.
\]
A finite set \(S\subset\N\) represents \(r\in\Q\) when every member of
\(S\) is a squarefree semiprime and
\[
             \sum_{n\in S}\frac1n=r.
\]
An \emph{avoiding} representation additionally requires
\(S\cap T=\varnothing\) for a prescribed finite obstruction set \(T\).

The avoiding formulation is not cosmetic.  It permits repeated use of a
unit-numerator theorem without reusing a denominator.  The formal proof has
four levels.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For squarefree \(b\ge3\) and every finite \(T\), construct a finite
      semiprime set \(S\), disjoint from \(T\), with
      \(\sum_{n\in S}1/n=1/b\).
\item Deduce the cases \(b=2\) and \(b=1\) by the identities
      \[
      \frac12=\frac13+\frac16,
      \qquad
      1=\frac12+\frac13+\frac16,
      \]
      choosing the component representations successively with avoidance.
\item For \(a\ge1\), inductively take \(a\) pairwise disjoint avoiding
      representations of \(1/b\), and unite them to obtain \(a/b\).
\item Order the final finite set, prepend the dummy value \(1\), and translate
      \(\operatorname{IsSemiprime}\) into
      \(\omega=\Omega=2\).
\end{enumerate}

Only step (i) is analytic.  The remainder of the article reconstructs it.

\section{The two explicit prime estimates}

Let \(\pi(x)\) denote the number of primes at most \(x\).  The released Lean
proof accepts exactly the following two nonstandard analytic statements.
They are transcribed in the release from \RS~\cite{RosserSchoenfeld1962}.

\begin{proposition}[\RS, Corollary 3, equation (3.8)]\label{prop:rs-dyadic}
For every real \(x\ge 41/2\),
\[
  \frac{3x}{5\log x}
   < \pi(\lfloor2x\rfloor)-\pi(\lfloor x\rfloor).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[\RS, Theorem 5, equations (3.17)--(3.18)]
\label{prop:rs-mertens}
There is a real constant \(B\) such that, for every real \(x\),
\[
 x>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \log\log x+B-\frac1{2(\log x)^2}
 <
 \sum_{\substack{2\le p\le\lfloor x\rfloor\\p\ {\rm prime}}}\frac1p,
\]
and
\[
 x\ge286\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{\substack{2\le p\le\lfloor x\rfloor\\p\ {\rm prime}}}\frac1p
 <
 \log\log x+B+\frac1{2(\log x)^2}.
\]
The same constant \(B\) occurs in both inequalities.
\end{proposition}

No prime number theorem or asymptotic Mertens theorem is an axiom of the
release.  The two propositions above are the complete released analytic
trust boundary.

For \(k\in\N\), write
\[
  P_k=\{p\text{ prime}:2^k\le p<2^{k+1}\}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:rs-dyadic}, applied with \(x=2^k\), yields for \(k\ge5\)
the explicit lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-density}
       |P_k|\ge \frac{2^k}{2\log(2^k)}.
\end{equation}
The constant is weakened slightly from the direct substitution in order to
obtain a convenient uniform interface.

The second input is used at two endpoints.  Subtracting the upper estimate at
\(2^{k_0}\) from the lower estimate at \(2^{3k_0+1}\) cancels \(B\).
For all sufficiently large \(k_0\)---the formal proof takes an explicit
threshold---one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cumulative-mertens}
 \sum_{k=k_0}^{3k_0}\ \sum_{p\in P_k}\frac1p
   \ge \frac{21}{20}.
\end{equation}
The proof checks the logarithmic gain and the two explicit error terms
numerically.

Let
\[
        \mathcal P(k_0)=\bigcup_{k=k_0}^{3k_0}P_k.
\]
Put \(A=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P(k_0)}1/p\) and
\(A_2=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P(k_0)}1/p^2\).  Unique factorization and the
diagonal/off-diagonal decomposition give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-identity}
 \sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathcal P(k_0)\\p<q}}\frac1{pq}
        =\frac{A^2-A_2}{2}.
\end{equation}
Since \(A\ge21/20\) by \eqref{eq:cumulative-mertens}, while the elementary
telescoping estimate
\[
       A_2\le\sum_{n\ge2^{k_0}}\frac1{n^2}
            \le\frac1{2^{k_0}-1}
\]
is small, the formal development derives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-mass}
 \sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathcal P(k_0)\\p<q}}\frac1{pq}\ge\frac12
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large \(k_0\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:dyadic-density}
provides the cardinality needed for the control system and the gadgets;
equations~\eqref{eq:cumulative-mertens}--\eqref{eq:pair-mass} provide the
reciprocal mass needed for the residual batch.

\section{The finite semiprime system}

Fix a squarefree integer \(b\ge3\) and a finite obstruction set
\(T\subset\N\).  All scales below are chosen after \(b\) and \(T\), and may
therefore be made arbitrarily large.

\subsection{Prime blocks and the common period}

Choose a large lower scale \(k_0\) and a larger terminal scale \(K\).
For each \(k_0\le k\le K\), choose a finite prime block
\(P_k\subset[2^k,2^{k+1})\) satisfying the density supplied by
\eqref{eq:dyadic-density}.  Let
\[
        \mathcal B=\bigcup_{k=k_0}^{K}P_k
\]
be the block support.  The scale is taken so large that every
\(s\in\mathcal B\) exceeds \(b\) and every element of \(T\).
Thus every block prime is coprime to \(b\).

The common Fourier period is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:period}
        L=b\prod_{s\in\mathcal B}s.
\end{equation}
Each denominator constructed below divides \(L\).

\subsection{Control edges}

The block system contains two kinds of ordered prime pairs:
pairs of distinct primes within one block, and selected bipartite pairs
between adjacent blocks.  Their products form the control set
\[
       E_{\mathrm{ctrl}}=\{pq:(p,q)\text{ is a control pair}\}.
\]
Every control edge is a product of two distinct primes.  The product map is
injective on ordered control pairs, so no multiplicity is lost when the pair
system is replaced by a finite set of denominators.

For a residue assignment \(a=(a_p)_{p\in\mathcal B}\), the control quadratic
energy is a sum of squared circular distances attached to these pairs.  For
an actual frequency \(h\), the assignment is \(a_p=h\bmod p\), and the
formal identity identifying pair energy with denominator energy gives
\[
       Q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(a(h))\le Q_E(h)
\]
whenever the final edge set \(E\) contains \(E_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\).
This inequality is the bridge from the combinatorial block system to the
Fourier minor-arc estimate.

The control reciprocal load can be made small by increasing \(k_0\), while
its reciprocal-square load remains quantitatively visible.  Write
\[
       \sigma_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^2
            =\sum_{e\in E_{\mathrm{ctrl}}}\frac1{e^2}.
\]
The number of internal pairs in the bottom block and
\eqref{eq:dyadic-density} give a lower bound of the correct scale,
\[
       \sigma_{\mathrm{ctrl}}
          \gg \frac{2^{-k_0}}{k_0},
\]
with an explicit weakened constant in Lean.  This lower bound later prevents
the main-label window from becoming too large compared with the edge sizes.

\subsection{Denominator gadgets and the squarefree hypothesis}

Let
\[
       R=\{r:r\text{ is prime and }r\mid b\}.
\]
Choose \(G\) distinct primes \(S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_G\}\) in a high block,
for example at scale \(2k_0\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:dyadic-density} guarantees
their existence once \(k_0\) is large.  The gadget edges are
\[
       E_{\mathrm{gad}}=\{rs:r\in R,\ s\in S\}.
\]
Because block primes exceed \(b\), one has \(r<s\), and every gadget is a
squarefree semiprime.  Also \(rs\mid L\).

Here the squarefree hypothesis is structural, not merely inherited from the
necessary condition.  If integers \(h,m\) agree modulo every prime divisor
of a squarefree \(b\), then they agree modulo \(b\).  Consequently, if \(h\)
and \(m\) agree on all block-support residues but do not agree modulo the
global period \(L\), then there is a prime \(r\mid b\) for which
\[
                   h\not\equiv m\pmod r.
\]
The implication fails for nonsquarefree moduli: agreement modulo every prime
divisor of \(4\), for instance, does not imply agreement modulo \(4\).
This is the precise point at which squarefreeness enters the extra-minor-arc
argument.

\subsection{The residual mass batch}

Let the \emph{base edges} be
\(E_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\cup E_{\mathrm{gad}}\).  By taking \(k_0\) large, their
reciprocal load is made smaller than \(3/(2b)\).  The candidate mass pool
consists of products \(pq\) with \(p<q\) in
\(\mathcal P(k_0)\), after removing finitely many forbidden or conflicting
edges.  The full pool has load at least \(1/2\) by
\eqref{eq:pair-mass}; the formal forbidden-budget estimates show that the
remaining pool still supplies the required residual mass.

A finite greedy lemma is used in a particularly robust form.  If a finite
set of positive summands has total mass at least a target and every individual
summand is smaller than a prescribed gap, a subset can be chosen whose sum
lies between the target and target plus the gap.  Applied to reciprocal
semiprimes, it gives a mass batch \(Q\) disjoint from the base edges and from
\(T\) such that the final edge set
\[
             E=E_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\cup Q\cup E_{\mathrm{gad}}
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:load-window}
       \frac{3}{2b}\le\sum_{e\in E}\frac1e<\frac3b.
\end{equation}
All edges are distinct squarefree semiprimes, avoid \(T\), and divide \(L\).

Define the common Bernoulli parameter
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
       \theta=\frac{1/b}{\sum_{e\in E}1/e}.
\end{equation}
The load window gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-window}
       \frac13\le\theta\le\frac23,
       \qquad
       \sum_{e\in E}\frac{\theta}{e}=\frac1b.
\end{equation}
The strict upper bound in \eqref{eq:load-window}, together with \(b\ge3\),
also gives
\[
                   \sum_{e\in E}\frac1e<1.
\]
This is the no-wrap condition needed to turn a congruence modulo \(L\) into
an exact reciprocal-sum equality.

\section{The weighted circle method}

\subsection{The weighted count}

Select each \(e\in E\) independently with probability \(\theta\).  For
\(A\subseteq E\), put
\[
       w(A)=\prod_{e\in A}\theta\prod_{e\in E\setminus A}(1-\theta).
\]
The weighted count
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wcount}
 W(E,\theta,b)
  =\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq E\\\sum_{e\in A}1/e=1/b}}w(A)
\end{equation}
is nonnegative.  If it is positive, at least one subset \(A\) supplies the
desired avoiding representation of \(1/b\).

Since every \(e\mid L\), the condition in \eqref{eq:wcount} can be tested
modulo \(L\).  The no-wrap inequality ensures
\[
  \sum_{e\in A}\frac1e=\frac1b
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sum_{e\in A}\frac{L}{e}\equiv\frac{L}{b}\pmod L.
\]
Finite Fourier orthogonality on \(\Z/L\Z\) therefore gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier}
 L\,W(E,\theta,b)
   =\sum_{h=0}^{L-1}
       \left(
       \prod_{e\in E}
          \left((1-\theta)+\theta
             \exp\!\left(2\pi i\,\frac{h}{e}\right)\right)
       \right)
       \exp\!\left(-2\pi i\,\frac{h}{b}\right).
\end{equation}
The proof now separates a symmetric set of main frequencies from its
complement.

\subsection{Variance and the main arcs}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma}
       \sigma_E^2
          =\sum_{e\in E}\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}{e^2}.
\end{equation}
Because \(\theta\in[1/3,2/3]\) and \(E\ne\varnothing\), this variance is
positive.  The control edges give the lower comparison
\[
       \sigma_E^2\ge\frac29\,\sigma_{\mathrm{ctrl}}^2.
\]
The non-control edges are chosen sufficiently large that their
reciprocal-square contribution is controlled, yielding a corresponding
upper comparison
\[
       \sigma_E\le 501\,\sigma_{\mathrm{ctrl}}
\]
in the released quantitative assembly.

Let
\[
      N=\left\lceil \frac{C}{\sigma_{\mathrm{ctrl}}}\right\rceil
\]
for a constant \(C\ge3\) selected after the global-control tail constants.
The main frequencies are canonically labelled by integers
\(-N\le m\le N\).  At label \(m\), the Fourier summand is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-term}
 T_m=
   \left(\prod_{e\in E}
      \bigl((1-\theta)+\theta\e^{2\pi i m/e}\bigr)\right)
      \e^{-2\pi i m/b}.
\end{equation}
For \(|m|/e\le1/10\), the logarithm of each Bernoulli factor has a verified
third-order expansion.  Summing it over \(E\), and using the exact mass
identity in \eqref{eq:theta-window}, cancels the linear phase against the
target factor:
\[
 \log T_m
   =-2\pi^2m^2\sigma_E^2+\delta_m,
 \qquad
 |\delta_m|
   \le 100000\sum_{e\in E}\left|\frac m e\right|^3.
\]
The large-scale inequalities in the construction ensure that the final
remainder is at most \(1/10\) throughout the label window.  Hence
\[
      \Re T_m
        \ge 0.8\,\exp(-2\pi^2m^2\sigma_E^2).
\]
A finite Gaussian-sum lower bound and \(N\ge1/\sigma_E\) then imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-lower}
 \Re\sum_{|m|\le N}T_m
   \ge
   \frac{c_3}{\sigma_E},
 \qquad
 c_3=0.8\,\frac{\exp(-\pi^2/2)}2.
\end{equation}
Moreover \(T_{-m}=\overline{T_m}\), so the main sum is real.

\subsection{The block-minor lane}

For \(t\in\R\), let \(\|t\|\) denote distance to the nearest integer.  The
squared modulus of a Bernoulli factor is
\[
  \left|(1-\theta)+\theta\e^{2\pi i t}\right|^2
   =1-4\theta(1-\theta)\sin^2(\pi t).
\]
The formal minor-arc inequality weakens this to an exponential:
\[
  \left|\prod_{e\in E}
       \bigl((1-\theta)+\theta\e^{2\pi i h/e}\bigr)\right|
       \le
       \exp\!\left(-\frac{16}{9}Q_E(h)\right),
\]
where \(Q_E(h)\) is the sum of suitable squared circular distances.

A frequency is placed in the block-minor lane when its residue assignment on
the block support is outside the global-control main arc.  Since
\(Q_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(a(h))\le Q_E(h)\), the global-control partition theorem
bounds the sum of the exponential energies over all such assignments by
\[
 \frac{\eta+C_{\mathrm{tail}}
     \exp(-C^2(16/9)/2)}
      {\sigma_{\mathrm{ctrl}}}.
\]
The map from frequencies modulo \(L\) to assignments on the block-support
primes is not injective: the factor \(b\) in \eqref{eq:period} remains.
The proof therefore uses a bounded-multiplicity form of the estimate, with
multiplicity at most \(b\).  Parameters \(\eta\) and \(C\) are chosen so that
the resulting block-minor contribution is a small fixed fraction of
\(c_3/\sigma_E\).

\subsection{The extra-minor lane}

The remaining minor frequencies resemble a main label on every block prime.
Let \(h\) be such a frequency and let \(m\) be its block label.  It is not a
genuine main frequency, so \(h\not\equiv m\pmod L\).  By the squarefree CRT
step from Section~4.3, there is a prime \(r\mid b\) such that
\(h\not\equiv m\pmod r\), while for every gadget prime \(s\in S\),
\[
                   h\equiv m\pmod s.
\]
Each gadget edge \(rs\) then contributes genuine Fourier damping.  Uniformly
for \(\theta\in[1/3,2/3]\), the product over a \(G\)-element gadget reservoir
is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-damp}
 \left(\sqrt{1-\frac{8}{9r^2}}\right)^G
 \le
 \left(\sqrt{1-\frac{8}{9b^2}}\right)^G.
\end{equation}
The number of extra frequencies is bounded by \(b(2N+1)\): each block label
has at most \(b-1\) non-main siblings in the CRT fiber.  Since the base in
\eqref{eq:gadget-damp} is strictly less than \(1\), \(G\) is chosen so that
the entire extra-minor sum is another small fixed fraction of
\(c_3/\sigma_E\).  The dyadic prime-density estimate ensures that the chosen
high block contains \(G\) distinct gadget primes.

\subsection{Positivity}

The terminal parameter order is important.  First the universal
global-control and main-term constants are fixed.  Then \(G\) is chosen to
force the gadget damping.  Finally \(k_0\) is taken large enough to satisfy
simultaneously:

\begin{itemize}
\item the prime-cardinality requirements for the block system and gadgets;
\item the reciprocal-mass and forbidden-budget requirements for \(Q\);
\item avoidance of the prescribed finite set \(T\);
\item the control-load and reciprocal-square comparisons;
\item \(2N+1\le L\), so the main labels inject into residues modulo \(L\);
\item the per-edge and cubic Taylor bounds on the main arcs.
\end{itemize}

With these choices, the real lower bound
\eqref{eq:main-lower} strictly exceeds the norm of the two minor-lane sums.
The imaginary part of the main sum is zero.  Therefore the full Fourier sum
in \eqref{eq:fourier} has positive real part, and
\[
                       W(E,\theta,b)>0.
\]
A subset \(A\subseteq E\) with \(\sum_{e\in A}1/e=1/b\) follows.  Its
members are distinct squarefree semiprimes and avoid \(T\).  This proves the
analytic unit-numerator theorem for squarefree \(b\ge3\).

\section{From unit numerators to the headline theorem}

We now complete the arithmetic reductions.

\begin{proposition}[Avoiding unit fractions]\label{prop:unit}
For every squarefree \(b\ge1\) and every finite \(T\subset\N\), there is a
finite set \(S\) of squarefree semiprimes such that
\(S\cap T=\varnothing\) and
\[
                         \sum_{n\in S}\frac1n=\frac1b.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The case \(b\ge3\) is the circle-method construction.  For \(b=2\), choose
an avoiding representation of \(1/3\), then an avoiding representation of
\(1/6\) disjoint from both \(T\) and the first set, and use
\(1/2=1/3+1/6\).  For \(b=1\), successively choose disjoint avoiding
representations of \(1/2\), \(1/3\), and \(1/6\), and use
\(1=1/2+1/3+1/6\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Numerator reduction]\label{prop:numerator}
Let \(a\ge1\), let \(b\) be squarefree, and let \(T\subset\N\) be finite.
There is a finite set \(S\) of squarefree semiprimes, disjoint from \(T\),
with
\[
                         \sum_{n\in S}\frac1n=\frac ab.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Induct on \(a\).  For the successor step, apply the induction hypothesis to
obtain \(S\), and apply Proposition~\ref{prop:unit} with obstruction
\(T\cup S\) to obtain a representation \(U\) of \(1/b\).  Then \(S\cap U\)
is empty and
\[
        \sum_{n\in S\cup U}\frac1n
          =\frac ab+\frac1b=\frac{a+1}{b}.
\]
\end{proof}

For a positive rational \(q\), Lean's reduced numerator and denominator
satisfy \(q=a/b\) with \(a>0\), \(b>0\), and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:numerator}, with \(T=\varnothing\), yields a nonempty
finite set \(S\).  Ordering \(S\) increasingly produces the denominators in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  The formal lemma for semiprimes proves
\(\omega(n)=\Omega(n)=2\), and the sum is transported from the finite set to
the ordered index interval.
