\begin{abstract}
We prove that a positive rational number can be written as a finite sum of
reciprocals of distinct squarefree semiprimes if and only if its reduced
denominator is squarefree.  The analytic input is the prime number theorem,
which by Abel summation yields a uniform supply of primes and reciprocal-prime
mass in a suitable range of dyadic intervals.  The rest of the proof is
finite.  A graph on the dyadic prime blocks controls low-energy residue
assignments, while a level-set estimate and a Gaussian tail bound the remaining
assignments.  A separate family of semiprimes supplies the required reciprocal
mass, and auxiliary denominators associated with the prime divisors of the
given denominator suppress the remaining minor frequencies.  Finite Fourier
inversion then produces an avoiding representation of the reciprocal of every
squarefree denominator; elementary reductions give the result for all positive
rationals.
\end{abstract}
