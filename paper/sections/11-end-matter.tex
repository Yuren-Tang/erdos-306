\section*{Formalization, finite certificates, and code availability}

A complete Lean~4 proof of the community formulation of Erd\H{o}s Problem~306
is archived on Zenodo \cite{TangErdos306}.  It is machine checked relative to
exactly two named nonstandard assumptions: Rosser--Schoenfeld Corollary~3,
equation~(3.8), and Theorem~5, equations~(3.17)--(3.18)
\cite{RosserSchoenfeld1962}.  Those assumptions provide the released analytic
backend.  The present article instead proves the common analytic interface from
the prime number theorem by Abel summation; this PNT-based provider and the
symbolic organization used here are not claimed to be formalized in the
archived release.  Their formalization is in progress for a later software
release and article update.

The downstream construction is mathematically the same finite mechanism.  The
archive also checks explicit finite closures for the local rigidity constants,
level-set charge rearrangements, inverse-square comparison, Taylor bounds,
terminal numerical budget, and finite-set-to-tuple bookkeeping.  In the article
these are separated from the conceptual parameters: for example,
$\mu=21/20$, $c_\sigma=1/100$, $C_2=1000001$, $K_\sigma=501$,
$r_T=\delta_T=1/10$, the coefficient $0.8$, equal quarter budgets, and $2004$
are admissible safety or finite certificates for the symbolic statements above.
The formal theorem represents the finite denominator set by a strictly
increasing tuple with an initial dummy value $1$ excluded from the reciprocal
sum; this is equivalent finite bookkeeping, not a stronger mathematical
endpoint.

The formalization was developed in connection with the Formal Conjectures
benchmark \cite{FormalConjectures2026}.  It supplies independently checkable
software evidence, but it is not used to replace any implication in the human
proof.
