\section{Reciprocal mass, weights, and variance}\label{sec:mass}

Let
\[
 \Pk(k_0)=\bigcup_{k=k_0}^{3k_0}\Pk_k,
 \qquad
 S_1=\sum_{p\in\Pk(k_0)}\frac1p,
 \qquad
 S_2=\sum_{p\in\Pk(k_0)}\frac1{p^2}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:inclusive-window}, these are the primes in
$[2^{k_0},2^{3k_0+1})$.  Expanding $S_1^2$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-pool}
 \sum_{\substack{p,q\in\Pk(k_0)\\p<q}}\frac1{pq}
 =\frac{S_1^2-S_2}{2}.
\end{equation}
The analytic interface gives $S_1\ge\mu>1$, while
$S_2\le(2^{k_0}-1)^{-1}\to0$.  Hence, after increasing $k_0$ so that
$S_2\le\mu^2-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pool-half}
 \sum_{p<q}\frac1{pq}\ge\frac12.
\end{equation}
Thus the structural requirement is $\mu>1$.  The choice $\mu=21/20$ and the
finite cutoff $k_0\ge5$ are one released certificate for
\eqref{eq:pool-half}.  All products are distinct squarefree semiprimes by unique
factorization.

The denominator family has three disjoint roles:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:denominator-decomposition}
 E=\Ectrl\dot\cup\Eaux\dot\cup\Emass.
\end{equation}
The control family has already been fixed.  The auxiliary family, defined in
Section~\ref{sec:minor}, is chosen after the required damping has been fixed but
before the mass family.  Its cardinality is fixed before $k_0$, and each of its
denominators contains a high-block prime; hence its reciprocal load tends to
zero.  Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:control-estimates}, this gives, for
the normalization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
 \alpha=\frac32,
\end{equation}
the eventual bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base-load}
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{base}}:=
 \sum_{e\in\Ectrl\dot\cup\Eaux}\frac1e
 <\frac\alpha b.
\end{equation}
Set $t=\alpha/b-\Lambda_{\mathrm{base}}>0$.

Remove from the pair family the denominators already used by the control and
auxiliary components and those in $T$.  At the final scale, every element of
$T$ lies below the pair scale.  The reciprocal load lost through the other
exclusions is at most $\Lambda_{\mathrm{base}}$.  Since $\alpha/b\le1/2$ for
$b\ge3$,
\[
 t+\operatorname{load}(\text{excluded pair terms})
 \le\frac\alpha b\le\frac12.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:pool-half}, this proves that the remaining pair family
has load at least $t$.  This is the exact forbidden-load cancellation; no
unstated surplus in the pair pool is used.

\begin{lemma}[Greedy reciprocal interval]\label{lem:greedy}
Let $(x_i)$ be a finite family of positive reals of total at least $t$, and
suppose $x_i<g$ for every $i$.  Some subfamily has sum in $[t,t+g)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a subfamily of minimal cardinality among those with sum at least $t$.
Removing any selected term drops the sum below $t$, so the original sum is less
than $t+x_i<t+g$.
\end{proof}

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy} with $x_e=1/e$, $g=\alpha/b$, and target $t$.
The bottom pair scale ensures $1/e<g$.  We obtain $\Emass$ such that, for
$\Lambda=\sum_{e\in E}1/e$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:load-window}
 \frac\alpha b\le\Lambda<\frac{2\alpha}{b}.
\end{equation}
Every denominator is a distinct squarefree semiprime, avoids $T$, and divides
\begin{equation}\label{eq:period}
 L=b\prod_{p\in\Bset}p.
\end{equation}
Define the common Bernoulli weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \theta=\frac{1/b}{\Lambda}.
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=3/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-window}
 \frac13<\theta\le\frac23,
 \qquad
 \sum_{e\in E}\frac\theta e=\frac1b,
 \qquad
 \Lambda<1.
\end{equation}
The middle identity is the exact expected-mass cancellation on the major arcs;
the last inequality gives \eqref{eq:no-wrap}.

The choice \eqref{eq:alpha} is one coherent normalization rather than several
unrelated constants.  In the admissible range $1<\alpha\le3/2$, the greedy
window gives $\theta\in(1/(2\alpha),1/\alpha]$.  The minimum of
$\theta(1-\theta)$ on that interval is maximized when its endpoint values are
balanced, which occurs uniquely at $\alpha=3/2$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vstar}
 v_*:=\min_{1/3\le u\le2/3}u(1-u)=\frac29.
\end{equation}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmaE}
 \sE^2=\sum_{e\in E}\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}{e^2}.
\end{equation}
Since $\Ectrl\subseteq E$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-lower}
 \sE\ge\sqrt{v_*}\,\sctrl.
\end{equation}
The noncontrol inverse-square contribution is finite on the common final scale.
More precisely, the dyadic reciprocal bounds control the pair component, and
the high-block prime in each auxiliary denominator supplies an additional
$2^{-2k_0}$ factor.  Hence there is a fixed finite constant $C_2$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-square-constant}
 \sum_{e\in E}\frac1{e^2}\le C_2\sctrl^2.
\end{equation}
Choose once and for all
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ksigma-choice}
 K_\sigma>\sqrt{C_2/4}.
\end{equation}
Since $\theta(1-\theta)\le1/4$, we obtain the symbolic variance comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-comparison}
 \sqrt{v_*}\,\sctrl\le\sE\le K_\sigma\sctrl.
\end{equation}
The archived finite instantiation uses $C_2=1000001$ and $K_\sigma=501$.
Finiteness of the comparison is structural; these integers are a checked
certificate and a rounded safety margin, respectively.
