\section{The two minor classes}\label{sec:minor}

Use canonical representatives $0\le h<L$.  By $2N+1\le L$, the residues of the
integers $|m|\le N$ are distinct.  Let $S_M$ be this genuine major-frequency
set and put
\[
 S_m=(\Z/L\Z)\setminus S_M.
\]
The partition is made only inside $S_m$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minor-partition}
 S_{\mathrm{blk}}
 =S_m\cap\{h:a(h)\notin\mathfrak M(C)\},
 \qquad
 S_{\mathrm{sib}}
 =S_m\cap\{h:a(h)\in\mathfrak M(C)\}.
\end{equation}
Thus $S_{\mathrm{blk}}\dot\cup S_{\mathrm{sib}}=S_m$; no genuine major
frequency is counted as a minor.

Put $P=\prod_{p\in\Bset}p$, so $L=bP$.  Frequencies with the same block
assignment differ by a multiple of $P$.  Inside $[0,bP)$ a fixed residue modulo
$P$ has at most the $b$ representatives
$r,r+P,\ldots,r+(b-1)P$.  This exact fibre factor, together with
\eqref{eq:control-energy-identity}, \eqref{eq:fourier-energy}, and Theorem
\ref{thm:global-partition} at $c=c_F$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-minor-budget}
 \sum_{h\in S_{\mathrm{blk}}}|F(h)|
 \le
 \frac{b\bigl(\eta+C_{\mathrm{tail}}(c_F)e^{-c_FC^2/2}\bigr)}{\sctrl}.
\end{equation}

Now let $h\in S_{\mathrm{sib}}$.  Choose a main-assignment label $m=m(h)$ with
\[
 |m|\le C/\sctrl\le N,
 \qquad h\equiv m\pmod p\quad(p\in\Bset).
\]
Because $h\notin S_M$, it is not congruent to $m$ modulo $L$.  If it agreed
with $m$ modulo every prime divisor of squarefree $b$, it would agree modulo
$b$; together with the block congruences and $(b,P)=1$, CRT would give
agreement modulo $L$, a contradiction.  Hence there is a prime
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mismatch-prime}
 r(h)\mid b,
 \qquad h\not\equiv m(h)\pmod{r(h)}.
\end{equation}
This is the second independent use of the squarefreeness of $b$.

After $C$ and the sibling damping target are fixed, choose one common set
$\mathcal G$ of exactly $G$ primes in a designated high block, all outside the
prime support of $b$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high-prime-separation}
 2N<s\qquad(s\in\mathcal G).
\end{equation}
The common analytic density threshold supplies the set.  Before the mass family
is selected, put into $\Eaux$ every product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aux-family}
 rs\qquad(r\mid b\text{ prime},\ s\in\mathcal G).
\end{equation}
Unique factorization recovers both primes, so these denominators are distinct
and cannot collide with products of two block primes.  One reservoir works
simultaneously for every possible mismatch prime dividing $b$.

Fix $h\in S_{\mathrm{sib}}$, write $m=m(h)$ and $r=r(h)$, and take
$s\in\mathcal G$.  Since $h\equiv m\pmod s$, write $h=m+js$.  The mismatch
modulo $r$ gives $j\not\equiv0\pmod r$, whence
\[
 \normdist{j/r}\ge\frac1r,
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{m}{rs}\right|<\frac1{2r}.
\]
The triangle inequality on $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ gives
$\normdist{h/(rs)}\ge1/(2r)$.  The one-factor identity therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aux-factor}
 \left|(1-\theta)+\theta e^{2\pi ih/(rs)}\right|
 \le\sqrt{1-\frac{4v_*}{r^2}}
 \le\beta_b,
 \qquad
 \beta_b:=\sqrt{1-\frac{4v_*}{b^2}}
 =\sqrt{1-\frac8{9b^2}}<1.
\end{equation}
The $G$ factors are distinct, so $|F(h)|\le\beta_b^G$.  There are at most
$2N+1$ labels and at most $b$ frequencies in each block fibre.  Hence the
second exact cardinality factor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sibling-count}
 |S_{\mathrm{sib}}|\le b(2N+1),
 \qquad
 \sum_{h\in S_{\mathrm{sib}}}|F(h)|
 \le b(2N+1)\beta_b^G.
\end{equation}
