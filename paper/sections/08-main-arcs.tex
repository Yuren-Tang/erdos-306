\section{Major frequencies}\label{sec:main}

Choose Taylor data
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Taylor-data}
 0<r_T<\frac14,
 \qquad
 0<\delta_T<\frac\pi2.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:major-constants}
 a_T=e^{-\delta_T}\cos\delta_T>0,
 \qquad
 c_{\mathrm{maj}}=\frac{a_T}{2}e^{-\pi^2/2}>0.
\end{equation}
For a cutoff $C\ge1$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N}
 N=\left\lceil\frac C{\sctrl}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
At the final scale we require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-geometry}
 2N<2^{2k_0},
 \qquad 2N+1\le L,
 \qquad \frac1{\sE}\le N,
\end{equation}
as well as the Taylor bounds below.

For $|m|\le N$, put
\[
 T_m=
 \prod_{e\in E}\bigl((1-\theta)+\theta e^{2\pi im/e}\bigr)
 e^{-2\pi im/b}.
\]
For real $z$, define $B_\theta(z)=(1-\theta)+\theta e^{2\pi iz}$.  If
$|z|\le r_T<1/4$ and $1/3\le\theta\le2/3$, then every factor stays in the open
right half-plane.  In particular it is nonzero, and the principal logarithm is
well defined along the whole segment from $0$ to $z$.  Uniformly on this compact
parameter set,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-expansion}
 \log B_\theta(z)
 =2\pi i\theta z
  -2\pi^2\theta(1-\theta)z^2+R_\theta(z),
 \qquad |R_\theta(z)|\le C_T|z|^3
\end{equation}
for a fixed $C_T$.

After increasing $k_0$, every $e\in E$ and $|m|\le N$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Taylor-fields}
 |m/e|\le r_T,
 \qquad
 C_T\sum_{e\in E}|m/e|^3\le\delta_T.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the smallest denominator tends to infinity, while
\eqref{eq:inverse-square-constant} controls the aggregate inverse-square mass;
the cubic sum is therefore uniformly small on the major window.

Summing \eqref{eq:log-expansion}, the linear term cancels exactly by
\eqref{eq:theta-window}.  Thus
\[
 \log T_m=-2\pi^2m^2\sE^2+\Delta_m,
 \qquad |\Delta_m|\le\delta_T.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:per-main}
 \Re T_m\ge a_Te^{-2\pi^2m^2\sE^2}.
\end{equation}
Conjugation sends $T_m$ to $T_{-m}$, so the major sum is real.  The integers
$|m|\le1/(2\sE)$ lie in the major window and each contributes at least
$a_Te^{-\pi^2/2}$.  A coarse integer count gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-lower}
 \Re\sum_{|m|\le N}T_m\ge\frac{c_{\mathrm{maj}}}{\sE}.
\end{equation}
The convenient choices $r_T=1/10$, $\delta_T=1/10$, and the lower coefficient
$0.8$ form one checked safety certificate; the proof uses only the positive
constants in \eqref{eq:Taylor-data}--\eqref{eq:major-constants}.

For every real $t$,
\[
 |(1-\theta)+\theta e^{2\pi it}|^2
 =1-4\theta(1-\theta)\sin^2(\pi t).
\]
On the centred interval $\sin^2(\pi t)\ge4\normdist t^2$, and
$\theta(1-\theta)\ge v_*$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier-energy}
 |F(h)|\le e^{-c_F\QE(h)},
 \qquad
 c_F=8v_*=\frac{16}{9}.
\end{equation}
Thus the Fourier damping exponent is derived from the weight normalization, not
chosen independently.
